Cartilage Repair & Transplant

Cutting-edge cartilage repair and transplant techniques learned through international fellowship training across Europe.

Restoring Cartilage for Lasting Relief

Articular cartilage — the smooth, white tissue that covers the ends of bones where they meet to form joints — is essential for pain-free movement. When damaged through injury or wear, cartilage has very limited ability to heal on its own. Without treatment, cartilage damage often progresses to arthritis.

Dr. Elguizaoui has specialized expertise in cartilage repair and transplant, developed through his international traveling fellowship at leading joint preservation clinics in Switzerland, the Netherlands, and Italy. He is a member of The New York Cartilage Repair Society and stays at the forefront of this rapidly advancing field.

Cartilage Repair Procedures

  • Microfracture — Stimulating the body's natural healing response by creating tiny holes in the bone beneath the damaged cartilage to promote new tissue growth
  • OATS (Osteochondral Autograft Transfer) — Transplanting healthy cartilage and bone plugs from a non-weight-bearing area to the damaged site
  • Osteochondral Allograft Transplant — Using donor tissue to replace larger areas of damaged cartilage and bone
  • Autologous Chondrocyte Implantation (ACI) — Growing your own cartilage cells in a lab and implanting them to regenerate damaged areas
  • Meniscal Allograft Transplant — Replacing a damaged or missing meniscus with donor tissue to protect the joint

When Is Cartilage Repair Recommended?

  • Focal cartilage defects from injury or trauma
  • Osteochondral lesions (damage to both cartilage and underlying bone)
  • Early arthritis in young, active patients
  • Cartilage damage associated with ligament or meniscus injuries
  • Failed previous cartilage procedures
  • Persistent knee or joint pain not responding to conservative treatment

Recovery & Outcomes

Recovery from cartilage repair varies depending on the specific procedure and location. Dr. Elguizaoui works closely with each patient to develop a customized rehabilitation program. Most cartilage procedures have excellent long-term outcomes, particularly in younger, active patients, often allowing return to high-level activities and sports.
